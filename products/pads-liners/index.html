Customization

What can be discussed for a private-label program.

Format, absorbency direction, topsheet, wrapper, count, retail pack, private-label cartons, and bundle kits are available for project discussion. The line sheet frames pad, liner, and accessory requirements as project-specific and bundle-ready. This does not mean everything is possible. Each item is reviewed against the intended market, sales channel, order quantity, quoted BOM, packaging artwork, and document scope.

Accessories can include storage pouches, wipes, sterilizing cups, and cleaning kits to build broader assortments for retail shelves, reusable-care bundles, travel kits, distributor catalogs, and regional trial programs. Bundle-ready SKUs may combine disposable formats with auxiliary items, but kit components, pack fit, and carton data must be reviewed as one program.

Material positioning and market-facing claims are confirmed only against the quoted BOM and supporting documentation. If a claim cannot be supported by the quoted BOM, packaging file, test file, or QC record, it should not be used in artwork or on-market copy.

Sample & Approval

From brief to approved production.

Sample review follows the OEM/ODM project path: brief and sample, customize and approve, trial production, then mass delivery. The first step confirms market, channel, product, volume, packaging, and document needs. The second step reviews product direction, color, packaging, artwork, and sample approval. The third step verifies dimensions, hardness or format, pack fit, process controls, and buyer records. Mass delivery then manages production, QC sampling, release files, cartons, and export handoff.

Key confirmation points before approval include format, material stack, pack fit, and QC checkpoints. Buyers should also confirm wrapper direction, retail count, box or pouch artwork, leaflet language, barcode, kit components, carton requirements, and document list.

Preconditions before mass production: a written production plan is only issued after product, artwork, packaging, document, inspection, and shipping details are agreed. No YUJI-specific lead time is stated in the line sheet. Industry references: catalog samples typically 7–14 days; paid sample sets 7–15 business days; pad mass production often 35–40 days after artwork and deposit. These are external reference values, not YUJI commitments.
