Buyer Summary

Confirm menstrual cup MOQ in a written per-SKU quote after size mix, color, packaging, artwork, document needs, and launch schedule are defined. Generic MOQ references are useful for budgeting and planning, but they are not binding. The written per-SKU quote from YUJI is the only binding number. Final market approval, label review, and regulatory clearance remain the buyer’s responsibility.

Industry-typical benchmarks—such as catalog OEM cups and discs starting around 500 units per SKU , custom printed packaging often starting at 500+ units per design , and new molds typically discussed at 500+ units —should be treated as planning ranges only. Actual MOQ depends on the exact SKU, tooling route, packaging structure, artwork status, document scope, and production schedule.

Use a written quote as the starting point, not a generic MOQ reference.

A useful menstrual cup MOQ answer starts with the launch model. Buyers who reuse an existing cup structure, keep packaging simple, and approve samples quickly can usually move faster than buyers who need new colors, custom molds, multi-language retail boxes, or broad document packs. This is why a generic MOQ number found online is rarely enough to place a purchase order. It does not know your size mix, your packaging artwork status, your target market, or your required launch date.

At YUJI, the practical starting point is a written per-SKU quote that states the assumptions behind the number. That quote should identify the cup structure, size range, color direction, logo placement, packaging format, artwork status, document list, sampling path, and estimated production schedule. Once those variables are fixed, the MOQ becomes a real planning figure rather than a rough benchmark.

MOQ — Confirm a written per-SKU quantity that identifies color, packaging, tooling, and customization assumptions. Project schedule — Confirm a dated production plan in the quote after approvals and delivery requirements are agreed. Size mix — The quoted SKU and dimension mix affects production planning and carton logic. Documents — Specs, certificate scans, QC release, and packaging files should be listed early. Written quote — The written per-SKU quote from YUJI is the only binding MOQ. This guide does not replace that quote.

For buyers, the safest approach is to request a written quote after the product and packaging brief is stable enough to review. If the brief is still changing, the supplier can only return a generic range. If the brief is clear, the supplier can identify the MOQ band, sampling path, lead time, and document requirements with much more confidence.

The key lesson is that cup MOQ is not one number. The written quote should separate the cup itself from the packaging and document scope so the buyer can see what is driving the quantity.

What changes menstrual cup MOQ?

Six drivers usually decide whether a cup project stays near a low trial band or moves into a higher custom-production band. The table below explains the practical impact of each driver.

MOQ driverPractical impact
Cup structureA quoted existing structure may be faster to evaluate than new mold development. If the shape, stem, rim, and suction-release design are already available, the buyer can focus on samples, color, and packaging instead of tooling.
Size mixA single-SKU trial differs from a multi-size assortment for retail or marketplace kits. Each additional size may add planning, carton, and inventory complexity, even when the cup body is similar.
Color and logoCustom colors and logo placement change material planning and approval steps. Color matching, logo durability, and decoration method can affect sample rounds and production minimums.
Packaging complexityPouch-only packs differ from retail box, leaflet, barcode, sterilizing cup, and starter-kit programs. Printed cartons, inserts, and multi-language leaflets often have their own MOQ triggers.
Sample approval pathFit, hardness, packaging copy, and artwork should be confirmed before larger production. Slow approvals or multiple sample rounds can push the production window later even if the unit MOQ does not change.
Compliance scopeDestination-market document needs can affect timing even when the unit MOQ stays similar. Buyers should confirm which specs, certificate scans, QC releases, and packaging files are required before the order is locked.

In practice, the most common surprise is packaging. A buyer may focus on cup MOQ, then discover that the retail box, leaflet, barcode, and language versions have their own minimums. Another common issue is custom color. A stock color may be planned with a lower risk profile, while a custom shade may require a material lot and a color approval step. The written quote should show these assumptions clearly.

Recommended path for a first cup launch: seven-step sequence

A first cup launch does not need to begin with a custom mold or a complex retail program. It should begin with a clear commercial plan and a controlled approval sequence.

  1. Define the target market, channel, and claim strategy. Confirm the destination country, sales channel, language needs, and any claim restrictions. This step determines packaging copy and document scope before artwork begins.
  1. Choose the cup structure path. Decide whether to evaluate an existing cup structure, a modified structure, or a new mold. An existing structure is often the fastest path for a trial order. A new mold may offer more differentiation but adds tooling, sampling, and validation time.
  1. Set the size mix and hardness direction. Decide which sizes are required and whether the cup should feel soft, medium, or firm. The size mix affects production planning, carton logic, and inventory. Hardness affects sample evaluation and material selection.
  1. Select packaging direction and artwork requirements. Choose pouch-only, retail box, leaflet, sterilizing cup, or starter-kit format. Confirm barcode, language, and artwork status. Packaging complexity often changes MOQ more than the cup itself.
  1. Request samples and a document list. Ask for samples that match the intended structure, size, and hardness direction. At the same time, request the document list for the target market so compliance planning happens early.
  1. Approve the golden sample and packaging proof. Confirm fit, hardness, color, logo, packaging copy, and artwork. Keep one approved reference for production. This reduces rework and helps prevent disputes later.
  1. Lock the written per-SKU MOQ, schedule, deposit, and QC plan. Once the sample and packaging proof are approved, confirm the written per-SKU MOQ, production lead time, payment terms, QC release, and shipping plan. The written quote from YUJI is the only binding MOQ.

This sequence keeps the project moving without pretending that every market, packaging format, or cup design can use the same MOQ. It also gives the buyer a clear approval trail.

Buyers should plan backward from the launch date. If the retail packaging is not approved, the production date cannot be final. If the mold is new, the T1/T2 samples and FAI sign-off must be completed before bulk production. If the market requires specific documents, those should be requested before the PO, not after the goods are ready.

Compliance documents and certification questions buyers should plan for

Compliance scope can affect timing even when the unit MOQ stays similar. Buyers commonly ask suppliers about ISO 13485 , ISO 9001 , ISO 10993 , CE technical-file support for Class I devices , FDA establishment registration rather than 510(k) , SGS per-lot reports , REACH , and RoHS . These are industry-typical buyer questions, not claims about YUJI’s certifications.

YUJI does not claim any specific certification in this guide. Certification status, document availability, and regulatory pathway must be confirmed in writing for the exact product, packaging, and destination market. The buyer is responsible for final market approval, label review, claims review, and any required registration or technical file. The supplier can support document preparation where applicable, but the written quote and compliance scope should define exactly what is included.

Frequently Asked Questions

What information should a menstrual cup RFQ include?

Target country and channel, cup sizes, hardness preference, color, logo placement, pouch or box structure, instruction language, sample quantity, launch date, first-order forecast, and the required document list. Adding mold status, artwork status, and packaging format helps the supplier return a more accurate MOQ.

What is the recommended path for a first menstrual cup launch?

Start with available cup structures and a clear size mix. Request samples with packaging direction, then confirm hardness, color, artwork, document scope, and written MOQ before locking the trial order. This sequence reduces rework and keeps approvals in a logical order.

Why is a generic MOQ number not enough?

A generic number does not know your size mix, packaging format, artwork status, document list, or launch date. Industry-typical ranges such as 500 units per SKU are useful for budgeting, but the written per-SKU quote from YUJI is the only binding MOQ.

What changes menstrual cup MOQ the most?

Cup structure, size mix, color and logo, packaging complexity, sample approval path, and compliance scope. New molds, custom colors, and multi-language retail packaging usually require more planning than a catalog cup with a simple pouch.

How do size mix and packaging affect MOQ?

Each size and color may be planned as its own SKU. A multi-size retail kit can be more complex than a single-SKU trial. Printed cartons, leaflets, barcodes, and multi-language artwork may also have their own minimums, often starting around 500+ units per design .

What certifications and tests do buyers commonly ask about?

Common questions include ISO 13485 , ISO 9001 , ISO 10993 , CE technical-file support, FDA establishment registration , SGS per-lot reports , REACH , and RoHS . Confirm the exact document list for your market in writing.

How long do samples and bulk production usually take?

Industry-typical sampling may run 7–14 days for catalog samples, 7–15 business days for paid samples, and 15–25 days for prototype-mold samples. Catalog OEM bulk production is often 25–40 calendar days after packaging approval and deposit. New-mold ODM may add 8–12 weeks for tooling and sampling.

Who is responsible for final market approval?

The buyer is responsible for final market approval, label compliance, claims review, and any required registration or technical file. The supplier can provide available documents and support, but the buyer should confirm the full compliance path before production.
