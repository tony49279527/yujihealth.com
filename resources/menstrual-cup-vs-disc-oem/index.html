Cup vs disc OEM: the practical difference

Menstrual cups are the mainstream reusable silicone starter SKU for DTC, Amazon, pharmacy pilots, and kit programs. They are familiar to many buyers and users, and they usually require moderate education around sizing, insertion, and care. That does not mean cups are “simple” in an OEM sense. They still need clear dimensions, capacity validation, material records, packaging artwork, and QC checkpoints.

Menstrual discs can support higher-capacity and premium positioning, but they usually need clearer insertion and removal education, stronger claim discipline, and more packaging support. Discs may also involve rim structure, return-feature design, and fit questions that are less familiar to first-time buyers. For that reason, a disc OEM program can be a strong premium play, but it is usually not the lowest-risk first reusable SKU unless the brand has an education plan.

Start with the hero SKU decision

The first decision is not “cup or disc?” in the abstract. It is “which product will be the hero SKU for this launch?” The hero SKU is the product that carries the launch story, packaging, sampling budget, and channel plan. If the hero SKU is unclear, cup and disc quotes may look comparable on paper but fail to answer the real business question.

If the brand needs a mainstream reusable entry product, start with cups. If the brand wants premium capacity positioning and can educate users, review discs. Some brands eventually launch both, but dual-SKU programs should follow a clear hero decision, not replace it.

Mainstream reusable: cup

A cup program usually fits brands that want a recognizable reusable format with broad channel potential. The buyer can focus on size range, silicone hardness, color, logo, pouch, box, leaflet, and kit configuration. The launch risk is lower for a first reusable SKU because the format is more familiar.

Higher-capacity premium: disc

A disc program fits brands that want to differentiate through capacity, comfort positioning, rim design, or premium kit presentation. The trade-off is education. Insertion, removal, and claim wording need more care. Packaging must often do more work, and the buyer should be ready to review instruction design and market claim language before artwork lock.

Cup OEM programs: the mainstream reusable starter

Cup capacity

Confirm quoted dimensions and verified capacity data for each proposed SKU. Capacity is not just a catalog number; it depends on the cup body, rim, fill line, and test method. Buyers should ask how capacity was measured and whether the quoted figure matches the intended market claim.

Cup capacity discussions should also connect to sizing. A cup range may include multiple sizes, and each size can have different capacity. If the launch plan includes more than one size, request a capacity reference for each proposed SKU, not a generic figure for the category.

Cup MOQ and customization

Cup MOQ should be confirmed in a written per-SKU quote after color, packaging, tooling, and customization are defined. Industry-typical catalog OEM cups and discs often start around 500 units per SKU. Custom printed cartons or pouches often start at 500+ units per design. New custom molds are usually discussed at 500+ units to amortize tooling. These are industry benchmarks, not YUJI commitments. YUJI’s actual MOQ must be confirmed per project.

Cup customization focus usually includes size, hardness, color, logo, pouch, box, and leaflet. Document focus usually includes material, biocompatibility-oriented records, EO plan, and QC release. Buyers should request the specific files available for the quoted SKU and manufacturing plan.

Disc OEM programs: premium capacity with education load

Disc capacity

Confirm quoted capacity, rim structure, and return-feature requirements. Disc capacity can be affected by rim design, basin shape, placement, and how the user wears it. A higher-capacity claim may sound attractive, but it must be supported by clear dimensions, test references, and user instructions.

Disc capacity also needs to align with the target market. Some channels expect detailed education; others expect simple packaging and a short instruction leaflet. The buyer should decide early how much education the package can carry and how much must live in digital content.

Disc MOQ and customization

Disc MOQ should be confirmed in a written per-SKU quote after structure, color, and pack plan are defined. As with cups, industry-typical reusable OEM MOQ often starts around 500 units per SKU. Rim or pull-ring tooling, custom packaging, and color matching can change the production plan. Buyers should not compare a cup quote and a disc quote unless both quotes define the same level of customization.

Disc customization focus usually includes rim feel, pull-ring design, color, and kit packaging. Document focus usually includes material, dimensional checks, packaging fit, and market claim review. Because discs are more education-dependent, packaging and leaflet review should happen earlier.

How to choose before requesting samples

1. Define the hero SKU

Write down whether the launch is cup-led or disc-led. If the brand needs a mainstream reusable entry product, start with cups. If the brand wants premium capacity positioning and can educate users, review discs. Do not let dual-SKU curiosity delay the hero decision.

2. Check packaging readiness

Cups often launch with pouch, box, and leaflet kits. Discs usually need clearer instruction design and claim review. Ask whether the current packaging concept can carry the required education without overcrowding the front panel. If not, adjust the pack plan before sampling.

3. Confirm MOQ logic

Ask for written cup and disc MOQ separately. Both can change with structure, color, packaging, tooling, and customization. Industry-typical MOQ may start around 500 units per SKU, but that figure is only a benchmark. YUJI must confirm the actual MOQ for the exact SKU and customization level.

4. Align documents early

Request material specs, available certificate scans, QC checkpoints, and packaging review files before artwork lock. For discs, include dimensional checks and claim review in the document plan. For cups, include sizing, capacity, and care instructions.

5. Decide whether to dual-SKU

Some buyers launch cups first, then add discs after the reusable category is established. Others launch a disc as a premium hero and add cups later as a volume SKU. Either path can work, but dual-SKU planning should not begin until the first product’s MOQ, packaging, and documents are understood.

Compliance boundary and document planning

Industry-typical certifications and tests buyers commonly ask about include ISO 13485, ISO 9001, ISO 10993 biocompatibility, CE technical-file support for Class I menstrual devices, FDA establishment registration — registration, not 510(k) clearance — SGS per-lot test reports, and REACH/RoHS. These are industry references, not claims about YUJI. YUJI does not claim to hold any certification unless it is confirmed in writing for the exact project. Document availability must be confirmed per project, per SKU, and per manufacturing plan.

Final market approval, registration, labeling, and market claims are the buyer’s responsibility. Buyers should review local requirements with their own regulatory counsel or agent. The OEM can support with requested documents where available, but the buyer owns the final market decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is easier for a first private-label launch: a menstrual cup or disc?

Menstrual cups are usually easier for a first reusable launch because buyers and users recognize the format. Discs can support premium positioning but need more insertion, removal, and packaging education.

Does a menstrual cup or disc have a lower OEM MOQ?

Confirm MOQ separately for cup and disc programs in a written per-SKU quote. Product structure, color, pull-ring design, packaging, tooling, and customization can change the production plan. Industry-typical reusable OEM MOQ often starts around 500 units per SKU, but YUJI’s actual MOQ must be confirmed per project.

What MOQ should buyers expect for reusable OEM?

Industry-typical catalog OEM cups and discs often start around 500 units per SKU. Custom printed cartons or pouches often start at 500+ units per design. New custom molds are usually discussed at 500+ units to amortize tooling. Treat these as benchmarks only.

Which documents should buyers compare for cup and disc OEM programs?

Compare material specifications, dimensions and capacity, available certificate or test scans, packaging requirements, sample approvals, and QC release checkpoints for the quoted product. If certifications such as ISO 13485, ISO 9001, ISO 10993 biocompatibility, CE technical-file support, FDA establishment registration, SGS per-lot test reports, or REACH/RoHS are requested, confirm availability per project.

How long do samples usually take?

Industry-typical catalog samples usually take 7–14 days. Paid samples often take 7–15 business days. Prototype-mold samples often take 15–25 days. YUJI must confirm timing per project.

How long does bulk production usually take?

Industry-typical catalog OEM bulk production often takes 25–40 calendar days after packaging approval and deposit. Approved tooling may take 30–45 days ex-works. Sanitary-pad mass production may take 35–40 days from order confirmation. Confirm per project.

Can a brand launch cups first and add discs later?

Yes. Some buyers launch cups first, then add discs after the reusable category is established. Others launch a disc as a premium hero and add cups later. The important point is to define the hero SKU before dual-SKU planning.

What RFQ information prevents misleading quotes?

Send target market, channel, preferred product format, capacity expectation, color, packaging language, sample quantity, first-order forecast, and document list. Also define structure, rim or pull-ring preference, hardness, kit packaging, and customization level. Without these fields, cup and disc quotes cannot be compared fairly.
