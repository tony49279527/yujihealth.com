Procurement Overview

China’s private-label feminine care supplier landscape.

China’s feminine care private-label market includes specialized silicone manufacturers for menstrual cups and discs, absorbent hygiene product factories for sanitary pads and liners, and full-range suppliers that produce both categories. Buyers launching a branded feminine care line should evaluate suppliers across product scope, material control, certification coverage, packaging integration, and quality system maturity.

Product scope. Confirm cups, discs, pads, liners, wipes, cleansing products, and accessories one SKU at a time. A broad catalog is not capability proof. Ask whether the supplier actually manufactures the quoted item, assembles it, sources it, or coordinates it through a partner.

Quality and market route. Determine intended use, classification, operator duties, claims, and applicable evidence by product. No single ISO , FDA , CE , or organic checklist covers all categories. The evidence package for a reusable silicone cup is not the same as the package for a disposable pad, a liner, or an accessory.

MOQ review. Confirm MOQ by product category and SKU, including packaging, tooling, and customization assumptions. Do not assume combined MOQ across products. A supplier may quote one MOQ for a catalog cup, another for a custom-printed pouch, and another for a new mold or a new pad specification.

Packaging. Confirm responsibility, artwork format, approval rounds, minimums, languages, barcode, carton data, and final release checks. Packaging is often where launch delays appear, especially when claims, translations, barcodes, and retail-ready carton data are approved late.

Official starting points: FDA registration and listing guidance and EU CE-marking guidance . Confirm the current SKU-specific route with the responsible regulatory parties. Market-entry and compliance responsibility stays with the buyer.

Supplier archetype 2: Category-specialized manufacturer

This archetype may suit a single-category program. Verify actual product expertise through the quoted specification, samples, process and QC records; do not assume specialization means stronger evidence, pricing, or compliance.

Best fit for: Buyers launching a single-category product line who want category-specific depth, specialized material expertise, and potentially lower pricing within that product type.

Procurement caution: Assess whether managing separate suppliers for each category aligns with your quality system, document management, and logistics resources. Multi-supplier programs require more coordination during sampling, production, and shipping. If you use one supplier for cups and another for pads, you may gain focus but add document handoffs, sample tracking, and packaging version control.

Supplier archetype 3: Diversified hygiene product group

This archetype may suit standardized, higher-volume programs. Verify the exact production line, quoted SKU, private-label minimum, changeover controls, evidence scope, and packaging flexibility rather than inferring fit from group size.

Best fit for: Retail chains and institutional buyers with high-volume forecasts and standardized product specifications who prioritize unit cost over format flexibility.

Procurement caution: Verify the manufacturer’s minimum engagement level for private-label programs versus branded production, confirm format and material change flexibility, and review whether feminine care receives dedicated production line allocation. A large group may have capacity, but your SKU still needs a named line, a named owner, and a documented changeover plan.

How to select the right private-label supplier for your program

Map your product roadmap. Define current and planned SKUs, but verify each new category independently before assuming a multi-category supplier reduces risk or switching cost. A supplier that performs well on a menstrual cup may not have the same absorbent hygiene controls, pad line schedule, or disposable packaging workflow.

Compare quality-system scope. If a certificate is relevant, verify holder, site, standard, covered activities or products, issue date, expiry date, and current status—not only a certificate number. For example, a quality-system document such as ISO 9001 or ISO 13485 may be relevant to some buyers, while material or biocompatibility evidence such as ISO 10993 may be relevant to others. The scope must match the quoted product and manufacturing site.

Evaluate packaging handoff. Confirm who owns design, revisions, languages, claims, barcode, print approval, carton data, and final inspection; do not request confidential client files. Your supplier should be able to explain how artwork moves from draft to print-ready file, who checks regulatory language, and how obsolete versions are blocked from production.

Review document management. Assess whether the supplier can provide consistent specification sheets, QC records, and certificate packages across all your product types. Build a document index with columns for file name, product, SKU, owner, status, issue date, expiry date where applicable, and verification notes.

Test communication and controls. Record RFQ clarity, open questions, document status, owners, and response dates; early responsiveness is useful evidence but not a production guarantee. A supplier that answers clearly during sampling is easier to manage during mass production, but you still need written QC checkpoints and batch traceability.

Frequently Asked Questions

What feminine care products can suppliers in China private-label?

Chinese suppliers may quote menstrual cups and discs, sanitary pads and liners, intimate-care formats, reusable pouches, starter kits, and related accessories. Buyers should confirm the exact product, dimensions, materials, claims, manufacturing route, and evidence scope for every quoted SKU.

How should buyers confirm MOQ for a feminine care private-label program?

Ask for a written MOQ by product category and SKU, identifying product structure, materials, color, packaging, tooling, and customization assumptions. Do not assume that a multi-product program has a combined or universal MOQ. Industry benchmarks often show cup/disc MOQ starting around 500 units per SKU , custom printed cartons or pouches at 500+ units per design , new custom molds discussed at 500+ units , and pads quoted around 300,000 pcs per SKU by some factories. Treat these as questions to verify, not YUJI commitments.

Can one supplier handle my full feminine care product range?

A supplier may quote multiple categories, but buyers should verify each product’s manufacturing route, specification, material, QC plan, evidence set, packaging responsibility, and market requirements separately. One company-level system or contact does not prove every SKU has the same controls or status.

Which certifications matter for private-label feminine care imports?

There is no universal certification list across cups, discs, pads, wipes, and accessories. Start with intended use, product classification, destination market, operator roles, and proposed claims. Then verify the exact quality-system, material, test, registration, or scheme evidence that applies. Industry-common items buyers often ask about include ISO 9001 , ISO 13485 , ISO 10993 , FDA establishment registration , CE technical file support for applicable Class I menstrual devices, REACH / RoHS lot reports, UKCA , and TGA . US registration is not FDA approval , and CE marking is used only when applicable EU legislation specifically requires it. YUJI’s document availability must be confirmed per project.

How do I evaluate a private-label feminine care supplier’s quality system?

Request the supplier’s quality-system certificate scope , such as ISO 9001 or ISO 13485 where relevant. Review sample QC records from recent production batches, ask about raw material inspection protocols, check whether in-process and final inspection checkpoints are documented, and request evidence of batch traceability from material receipt to shipment.

What information should I send for a private-label feminine care RFQ?

Send target market and channel, product type, quantity forecast per SKU, preferred materials or ingredients, packaging direction, color and logo requirements, sample quantity, launch timeline, and required certification or document list. Add your claim intentions, language requirements, barcode needs, and carton data so the supplier can quote packaging accurately.

What lead times and sampling steps should buyers plan for?

Catalog samples typically take 7–14 days . Paid sample sets of 3–10 pieces often ship within 7–15 business days . Prototype-mold samples commonly take 15–25 days . Cup catalog OEM bulk may run 25–40 calendar days , pads may run 35–40 days , catalog OEM with existing pack art may reach first ship in 4–8 weeks , and new mold ODM can add 8–12 weeks for tooling, T1/T2 samples, and approval loops. Confirm every date in writing before relying on it.

How should buyers manage packaging artwork, claims, and document ownership?

Assign owners for design, revisions, languages, claims, barcode, print approval, carton data, and final inspection. Keep a version-controlled document index and do not assume the supplier owns regulatory language or market claims. The buyer should approve final artwork, confirm market-specific requirements, and retain responsibility for market-entry and compliance decisions.
