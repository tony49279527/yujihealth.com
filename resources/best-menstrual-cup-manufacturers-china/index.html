Comparison Method

How this cup supplier shortlist was built.

This is a procurement shortlist, not a paid ranking or a guaranteed list of every qualified Chinese factory. It is based on the buying factors that change cup programs: material traceability, documentation depth, MOQ logic, packaging integration, and QC discipline.

Material and regulatory readiness

The quoted LSR specification, applicable finished-product or material tests, and whether each certificate, registration, or market claim matches the product and legal route.

Program and packaging fit

Size range, hardness options, color/logo support, pouch/box/leaflet capability, and whether the supplier can handle a starter kit or mixed reusable program.

Sample and QC discipline

Sample approval before artwork, inspection checkpoints, QC release records, and how clearly the supplier separates factory evidence from buyer-side compliance responsibilities.

Limits: no manufacturer profile is a substitute for current certificates, batch records, sample testing, and factory verification. Confirm every claim with the quoted supplier before commercial use.

How to choose the right manufacturer for your procurement stage.

  1. Define your volume tier. First-year forecasts under 10,000 units suit flexible MOQ suppliers. Multi-year volume commitments can unlock pricing at higher-output factories.
  2. Assess the legal route. Start with intended use, product classification, destination market, operator roles, and claims; then request only the documents that apply.
  3. Check packaging readiness. Factories with in-house packaging design reduce development cycles. Request packaging capability examples during supplier screening.
  4. Request QC records. Compare how each manufacturer documents dimensional checks, visual inspection, sterilization records, and batch release criteria.
  5. Verify communication flow. English-speaking project management and responsive RFQ handling reduce sampling and production delays.

Buyer FAQ

Common questions about menstrual cup manufacturers in China.

What is the typical MOQ for menstrual cup OEM from a Chinese manufacturer?

YUJI uses 1,000 pieces as a trial MOQ reference for menstrual cup programs. MOQ varies by manufacturer based on color count, size range, packaging complexity, and customization depth. Always confirm MOQ per SKU before quoting artwork and tooling.

Which certifications should a menstrual cup manufacturer in China hold?

Required evidence depends on the cup's intended use, destination market, operator roles, and proposed claims. Verify the quoted material specification and any applicable test reports. For the US, determine classification, product code, registration or listing duties, and any exemption limitations; registration is not FDA approval. For the EU, use CE marking only when applicable legislation specifically requires it.

Can a single Chinese manufacturer produce both menstrual cups and discs?

A supplier may quote both cups and discs, but buyers should verify each product's mold and process capability, material specification, QC plan, packaging support, and market-document route separately before combining the program.

How do I compare quotes from different menstrual cup manufacturers in China?

Compare quotes by reviewing material specification clarity, S/M/L sizing dimensions and capacities, hardness options, color and logo capability, packaging components (pouch, box, leaflet), QC release points, and the scope of every supporting document. Unit price alone is not a reliable comparison metric.

What is the typical lead time for a menstrual cup OEM order from China?

Use 15-20 working days only as a YUJI planning reference, not an industry promise. Confirm the production slot after sample, material, artwork, document, packaging, inspection, and shipping requirements are agreed.

Should I visit a menstrual cup factory in China before placing an order?

For higher-risk or volume programs, consider a factory visit or qualified remote audit. Review the applicable molding, inspection, and packaging areas and request approved sample records; do not treat a video or photograph as proof of certificate or product scope.
