Procurement Overview

China’s menstrual cup manufacturing landscape for buyers.

Buyers evaluating Chinese manufacturers should compare four dimensions: quoted material traceability and test scope, size and hardness control, packaging and any applicable sterilization route, and destination-market document responsibilities.

The manufacturers listed below represent different procurement profiles. No single factory fits every buyer. Use this comparison to identify which supplier matches your volume, market, and quality system requirements.

  • Material: Verify the quoted material identity, supplier and lot traceability, change control, and any test report required for the intended use.
  • Market route: Determine applicable classification, operator duties, and documents by product and destination; no badge is a universal export benchmark.
  • MOQ: No independent benchmark is asserted here. Compare written per-SKU MOQ, material minimums, tooling, size mix, and packaging minimums.
  • Lead time: Obtain a dated production-slot estimate tied to approved samples, materials, artwork, documents, inspection, and shipping.

Official starting points: FDA registration and listing guidance and EU CE-marking guidance . Confirm the current product-specific route with the responsible regulatory parties.

Supplier Archetype 2: High-volume silicone molding factory.

This archetype may suit a buyer with stable forecasts and in-house packaging or regulatory capability. Do not infer price, capacity, specialization, or document depth from factory size or location; verify the actual line, SKU, MOQ, changeover controls, and records in the quote and audit.

Best fit for: Established brands with high-volume forecasts who can manage packaging design in-house and need straightforward silicone molding at scale.

Procurement caution: Confirm the production areas used, material segregation and traceability, agreed QC methods, and whether any test report covers the quoted finished cup rather than a generic material or unrelated SKU.

Supplier Archetype 3: Documentation-intensive contract manufacturer.

This archetype may suit a project whose classification and risk require more formal design, test, change-control, or submission support. Verify the certificate holder and scope, product-specific responsibilities, approved evidence, commercial minimums, and whether the proposed regulatory route actually applies.

Best fit for: Medical device distributors, regulated-market entrants requiring full biocompatibility files, and buyers who need sterilization validation records.

Procurement caution: Assess whether the CMO’s minimum engagement level matches your forecast. Some CMOs require annual volume commitments that exceed first-year trial plans.

How to Choose the Right Manufacturer for Your Procurement Stage

Define your volume tier. Share first-year forecasts, reorder expectations, and channel plans, then compare each supplier’s written MOQ and production assumptions. A supplier that fits a trial launch may not fit a retail rollout.

Assess the legal route. Start with intended use, product classification, destination market, operator roles, and claims; then request only the documents that apply. Do not ask for a generic certificate pack and assume it covers your product.

Check packaging readiness. Factories with in-house packaging design reduce development cycles. Request packaging capability examples during supplier screening. If packaging is outsourced, confirm who owns artwork approval, dielines, barcode checks, and retail compliance.

Request QC records. Compare how each manufacturer documents dimensional checks, visual inspection, sterilization records, and batch release criteria. Ask whether QC records are tied to the quoted SKU or only to a generic process.

Verify communication flow. Record open RFQ questions, document status, owners, and response dates; early communication is useful evidence but not a production guarantee. A supplier that answers slowly before deposit may answer slowly during production.

Frequently Asked Questions

How should buyers confirm MOQ for a menstrual cup OEM program?

Ask each supplier for a written per-SKU MOQ that identifies size mix, color count, packaging components, tooling, and customization assumptions. Do not use a generic MOQ as a quotation or launch-plan commitment. Industry-typical catalog cup/disc MOQ may start around 500 units per SKU , but your exact MOQ must be confirmed in writing.

Which certifications should a menstrual cup manufacturer in China hold?

Required evidence depends on the cup’s intended use, destination market, operator roles, and proposed claims. Verify the quoted material specification and any applicable test reports. Commonly discussed items include ISO 9001 , ISO 13485 , ISO 10993 , FDA establishment registration , CE technical file support for Class I menstrual devices , REACH/RoHS , UKCA , and TGA . For the US, determine classification, product code, FDA registration or listing duties , and any exemption limitations; registration is not FDA approval . For the EU, use CE marking only when applicable legislation specifically requires it . YUJI’s document availability must be confirmed per project.

Can a single Chinese manufacturer produce both menstrual cups and discs?

A supplier may quote both cups and discs, but buyers should verify each product’s mold and process capability, material specification, QC plan, packaging support, and market-document route separately before combining the program. Do not assume cup capability automatically proves disc capability.

How do I compare quotes from different menstrual cup manufacturers in China?

Compare quotes by reviewing material specification clarity, quoted dimensions and capacity data, product-feel or measured hardness requirements, color and logo capability, packaging components, QC release points, and the scope of every supporting document. Unit price alone is not a reliable comparison metric. A lower unit price may exclude tooling, packaging, testing, or document support that your launch requires.

How should buyers confirm production timing for a menstrual cup OEM order?

Ask for a dated production plan after sample, material, artwork, document, packaging, inspection, and shipping requirements are agreed. A generic lead-time range is not a project commitment. Industry-typical cup catalog OEM bulk production may run 25–40 calendar days , but your actual schedule depends on approved samples, material availability, artwork, and line capacity.

Should I visit a menstrual cup factory in China before placing an order?

For higher-risk or volume programs, consider a factory visit or qualified remote audit. Review the applicable molding, inspection, and packaging areas and request approved sample records; do not treat a video or photograph as proof of certificate or product scope. If a visit is not possible, use a documented remote audit checklist and verify records against the quoted SKU.

What sample and QC records should a buyer request before approving a cup program?

Request sample dimensions, capacity data, hardness or product-feel reference, visual inspection criteria, material identity, and the proposed QC release format. If sterilization applies, ask how cycles and validation records are tied to the product. Confirm whether the records cover the quoted cup or only a generic material or unrelated SKU.

What should a buyer include in an RFQ to get comparable quotations from Chinese manufacturers?

Include target market, product format, dimensions, capacity direction, desired feel, color and logo needs, packaging format, estimated first-year volume, reorder expectations, document requirements, sample timing, and shipping destination. A complete RFQ reduces assumptions and makes supplier quotes easier to compare line by line.
